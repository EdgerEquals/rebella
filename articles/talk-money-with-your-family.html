How to talk money with your family

23 October 2026Your World

Money can feel like a grown-ups-only topic, but learning about it early is one of the most useful things you can do. Here is how to start the conversation without it feeling weird.

Pick a good moment

Not in the middle of a busy morning, and not straight after asking for something expensive. A car trip, a walk or helping cook dinner are all good times for a relaxed chat.

Conversation starters

  • "What was the first thing you ever saved up for?"
  • "How did you learn about money when you were my age?"
  • "Could I earn some money by helping out with extra jobs?"
  • "Can you show me how you plan the shopping budget?"
  • "What's one money mistake you wish someone had warned you about?"

Everyone's family situation is different, and some families don't like to talk about money. That's okay. Asking about how they learned, rather than what they earn, usually feels more comfortable.

Asking questions about money is a grown-up move.

Saving up for something

Choose a goal, work out the cost, and divide it into small weekly amounts. A clear jar, a chart on your wall or a simple spreadsheet lets you watch your progress grow. Some people split any money they get into three parts: spend, save and give.

Thinking about a first job?

Babysitting younger cousins, pet-sitting for neighbours you know, or helping out in a family business are common first steps. Rules about what age you can start paid work, and what kinds of jobs are allowed, depend on which Australian state or territory you live in. Check with a parent or carer, and look at the Fair Work Ombudsman website (fairwork.gov.au) for information about young workers.

Learn more

The Australian Government's free Moneysmart website (moneysmart.gov.au) has easy guides on budgeting, saving and banking. Explore it with an adult.
