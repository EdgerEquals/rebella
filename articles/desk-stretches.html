Stretches you can do at your desk

23 October 2026Mind & Body

Homework, gaming, scrolling: we spend a lot of time sitting still. These gentle stretches take a couple of minutes and you don't even have to leave your chair.

Move slowly, breathe normally and never stretch to the point of pain. A stretch should feel like a gentle pull, not a hurt. If anything is sore or you have an injury, check with a parent, carer or doctor first.

1. Neck tilts

Sit tall. Slowly tilt your right ear towards your right shoulder and hold for a few breaths. Come back to the middle and repeat on the left. Keep your shoulders relaxed and down.

2. Shoulder rolls

Lift your shoulders up towards your ears, roll them back and down, then forwards. Do five big slow circles each way.

3. Wrist and finger stretch

Hold one arm out in front with your palm facing away, like a stop sign. Use your other hand to gently draw your fingers back towards you. Hold, then swap. Finish by making fists and spreading your fingers wide a few times. Great after lots of typing or drawing.

Your body was made to move, not to stay frozen in one position all day.

4. Seated twist

Sit with both feet flat on the floor. Place your right hand on the outside of your left knee and gently turn your upper body to the left. Hold for a few breaths, come back to centre, and twist the other way.

5. Ankle circles

Lift one foot off the floor and draw slow circles with your toes, five each way. Swap feet.

6. Reach for the sky

Stand up if you can, link your fingers and stretch your arms up over your head. Take a big breath in and let it out slowly.

Make it a habit

Try stretching at the end of each study block, or whenever you finish a chapter or a level. Getting up for a glass of water or a quick walk around the house counts too.

Need to talk? You're not alone.

Talk to a parent, carer, teacher or school counsellor, or contact one of these free Australian services. Rebella isn't a medical service and can't give medical advice.

  • ReachOut: online support, tips and stories for young people
  • Kids Helpline: call 1800 55 1800 any time, for any reason, or chat online
  • headspace: mental health support for 12 to 25 year olds, online and in centres

If you or someone else is in danger right now, call 000.
