Cookies and your device

What websites save in your browser, what Rebella saves (not much), and how to clear it.

What is a cookie?

A cookie is a little note a website saves in your browser. Next time you visit, your browser hands the note back to that website.

Cookies can be handy. They can keep you signed in, or remember what's in a shopping cart.

Cookies and local storage: what's the difference?

Both are little notes kept in your browser. The difference is who gets to read them.

  • Cookies are sent back to the website's computers every time you visit a page.
  • Local storage stays put in your browser. The page can read it, but it isn't sent anywhere unless the website's code chooses to send it. Rebella's code never does.

What Rebella saves

Rebella doesn't set any cookies. It uses local storage for a few small things:

  • Your nickname, if you choose one at the bottom of the page. It's saved with the date you saved it, and that's all.
  • Your mood board, if you press Save on the Create page, so it's still there next time.
  • Pause motion, if you press it at the bottom of the page, so things stay still next time too.
  • The Perth weather shown at the top and bottom of the page, kept for half an hour so it isn't fetched on every page.

They all stay on this device, in this browser. They're never sent to us or to anyone else. Tap "forget my name" or Clear on the Create page, or clear your browser's site data, and they're gone.

When the Music Playground opens, it will remember your sound settings in the same way: on your device only.

Things other websites might save

A few parts of Rebella come from other websites:

  • Fonts come from Google Fonts. Your browser asks Google for the letter shapes. Google says it doesn't set cookies for this.
  • A podcast player on one dinosaur article comes from Captivate. Once it loads, it can save its own notes, under its own rules.
  • YouTube videos. Where Rebella shows a video, it uses YouTube's privacy-enhanced mode. YouTube says it doesn't save cookies until you press play. Once a video plays, YouTube may save its own notes in your browser, under YouTube's rules.
  • The weather at the bottom of the page comes from Open-Meteo, a free weather service. Your browser asks it for Perth's weather only. Rebella never asks where you are, and no cookies are used.
  • The weather chip at the top of the page is a link to the Bureau of Meteorology's Perth forecast. Nothing is sent to them unless you tap it, and it opens in a new tab. The greeting and the emoji next to it are worked out on your device.

Why do some sites use tracking cookies?

Some websites, often ones with ads, use cookies to notice which pages you visit, sometimes across lots of different sites. That helps them guess what you like, so they can show you ads.

Clues that a site uses them:

  • A banner asking you to "accept all cookies", with words like "partners", "advertising" or "personalised ads".
  • Ads that follow you around, like seeing the shoes you looked at on a totally different site.
  • A long list of company names in the cookie settings.

You can usually say no to tracking and the site still works. It's your choice what feels okay to share.

How to clear cookies and site data

  • Chrome (computer or Android): Settings, then Privacy and security, then Delete browsing data. Tick "Cookies and other site data".
  • Safari on iPhone or iPad: Settings app, then Apps, then Safari, then Clear History and Website Data.
  • Safari on a Mac: Safari menu, then Settings, then Privacy, then Manage Website Data.
  • Firefox: Settings, then Privacy & Security, then Cookies and Site Data, then Clear Data.
  • Edge: Settings, then Privacy, search, and services, then Clear browsing data.

Clearing signs you out of other sites too, so it's a good idea to ask a grown-up first if it's a shared device.

Want to just clear Rebella's notes? Use "forget my name" at the bottom of any page, and Clear on the Create page.
